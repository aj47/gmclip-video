# 0053 — Previews that match the export, guarded; trims and `see` that show what they can

Date: 2026-10-26 · Decided by: agent (go from AJ on 2026-10-26: "previews that match the export frame for frame") · Status: adopted

## Context
R-COMP-1 was proven on AJ's media only: `rcomp1_lab` (50 frames each of BENCH-60, the golden cuts and h79) and 517 frames of (a′) played in order (`milestones/m2/perf.md`, lane VT). Every one of those tests is `lab` and skips when its media is gone; half already do. Playback's sound was said to be "bit-identical to the export's mix" (`playback.rs`) with no test at all, and multi-source audio is about to change both mixes.

## Decision
- **Standing guard.** A fixture cut made at test time from two ffmpeg-made sources (`crates/gmclip-render/tests/identity`): six parts with a cross-source join, a jump forward past a keyframe, a jump back, a continuous join and a part whose picture and sound come from different sources; a caption and a gain envelope each cross a join. It runs wherever export runs (VideoToolbox and Metal), without `lab` and without AJ's media; skipped only without ffmpeg, as `rate_conversion`.
  - **Frames:** a canvas-size preview session seeks to both sides of every join, plays the whole cut in order, and does both again after `set_plan` from the cut before a trim. Every frame's NV12 equals the export's encoder input.
  - **Sound:** `ExportOptions::hash_encoder_input` also records the mix before loudness, hashed per 1024 samples (`ExportReport::mix_hashes`, `export::mix_block_hashes`). The app's playback engine runs headless on the mix it plays (`playback::plan_mix`). A play of the whole cut hears that mix bit for bit, except the play's own 64-frame de-click ramps.
  - A feature that changes what preview or playback draws or mixes is added to the fixture plan.
- **Finding: after a seek, playback is within 10⁻⁴ of the export, not bit-identical.** An AAC reader that re-seeks restarts decoding at another packet than the export's sequential read did, and AudioToolbox's output keeps a trace of where it restarted: ≤ 1.1·10⁻⁵ (−99 dBFS) on the fixture, inaudible. A play that starts mid-cut is held to 10⁻⁴ (−80 dBFS). A sample of offset, a 0.01 dB gain step or a missing fade are all well above that. The paused frame and the encoder input remain byte-identical.

- **Trim monitor.** While a part edge is dragged on the timeline, the viewer shows, paused, the frame on the moved edge: the part's new first frame (in) or last frame (out), as the trim would leave it. The cut is planned with the trim applied to a copy of the document (`AppCore::trim_peek`, the same source range `cut_trim` commits), so a lengthened edge shows source frames the cut does not have yet; nothing is committed or logged. One request per animation frame, the latest wins. A readout by the edge says how far it moved ("+4f", "−12f"). Release or Esc puts the viewer back on the cut at the frame it showed before the drag; the release's trim then lands as one command, as before.
- **Edits while playing reach the sound in time.** Before, a commit during a play re-planned the picture but the sound went on in the old plan until the play restarted, so the next join played the old arrangement. Now the viewer keeps the frame on screen (the same source frame, moved with its part: `viewer::same_frame`) and retargets the play (`Audio::retarget`): the device keeps what is queued up to two chunks past the one being filled (~40 ms), skips the rest, and the producer mixes it again from the edited plan right behind it, with no break and no fade. The clock keeps the play's numbering plus the edit's shift. Ranges and loops play again from the playhead. The mix itself is untouched.
- **A trim drag never stretches the part's drawing.** The waveform is drawn at the timeline's scale on the part's own frames, clipped to the dragged shape: shortening cuts it off at the edge, and lengthening draws the part's level on its own source past the edge (`timeline_part_energy`, the `energy` column's hops, fetched on the grab). Thumbnails show only the part's own frames (a lengthening drag no longer shows a neighbour's).
- **`see` shows what it can.** `see` planned the whole cut, so one planner refusal (a caption with no style) failed every framing check. Now, when the cut does not plan, `see` plans it without its overlays, caption tracks and caption cues and adds them back (each kind whole, then in halves) while it still plans; what does not is left out of the frames and listed in `skipped` (`element`, `id`, the planner's `why`). A cut that does not plan even without them is refused as before. Export, lint and verify plan the whole cut, unchanged.

## Evidence
- `crates/gmclip-render/tests/preview_export_identity.rs`: 140 frames, joins at 24, 48, 72, 92, 116; 0 differ at seeks, playing, or after an edit; 219 mix blocks equal. With a one-frame-early picture at frame 48 it fails at 48 in all four passes.
- `app/src-tauri/tests/playback_export_identity.rs`: whole play 0 of 224 000 samples differ; a range play across two joins is at worst 1.09·10⁻⁵ from the export; 0 underruns. A 0.1 % gain on the mono path's right channel fails it in both plays.

- `app/ui/tests/e2e/trimmonitor.spec.ts`: "+8f" with the viewer on the part's new last frame of a cut 8 frames longer, "−5f" on the frame 5 before the old end, then back on the frame before the drag after release and after Esc. Without the timeline change, the readout never appears.
- `app/src-tauri/tests/playback_edit_while_playing.rs`: an edit after the playhead, and one that also deletes the part before it, retargeted 50–400 ms before a join. Shortest lead honoured: 100 ms (it was 250 ms when only chunks not yet queued were mixed again, and never before this change); 0 underruns and 0 samples off the old plan before the join at every lead.
- `app/ui/tests/e2e/trimwave.spec.ts`: the waveform pixels over the middle of part 1 are the same before the drag, 80 frames shorter and 80 frames longer; the revealed 80 frames are drawn. Before, shortening alone changed them.
- `crates/gmclip-mcp/tests/media_tools.rs::see_leaves_out_what_the_planner_refuses_and_says_so`: two text overlays, one the planner refuses; `see` renders with the other and lists the refused one with its reason, and `export` still answers `Unsupported`. Without the change `see` answers `Unsupported`.
