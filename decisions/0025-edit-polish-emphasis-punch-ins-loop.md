# 0025 — Edit polish: stressed words, punch-ins, callouts, the loop option, holds where the screen jumps

Date: 2026-10-16 · Decided by: agent (lane K2; AJ's "Ideas" picks of 2026-10-16, built with defaults while he was away) · Status: adopted, AJ may veto

## Context
AJ's rules for shorts include: 1–3 word captions with the key words in yellow, a cut every 3–9 s that is never jumpy, one steady layout, a last frame that loops back to the opening, the screen held on its last relevant frame when it jumps to something unrelated mid-sentence, and numbers from the footage. He picked five polish ideas on 2026-10-16. Each is a suggestion that says what it measured, one click to apply, one undo step.

## Decision
- **Stressed words** (`gmclip_analysis::emphasis`): a caption word is stressed when it is louder or slower than the speaker's **running baseline in that cut**. The baseline is the median and MAD of the ±20 words around it, so the thresholds are z-scores and never fixed dB. Level is the mean power of the loudest half of the word's level frames, plus the part's gain. Pace is ms per syllable. A word before a pause or a sentence end is compared only with other such words, because phrase-final lengthening is not stress. A word is proposed at z ≥ 2. At most one word in ten is proposed, never two within four words. Fillers and weak words are never proposed. The words are proposed as keywords (`caption_keywords_mark`, one step). Level comes from the stored level frames of the source over each word's own span. That is the cut's own audio for every part that plays its source as recorded (decision 0022's rule); a part with an envelope is measured as recorded, which is a known limit.
- **Automatic emphasis is the caption style's choice**: `CaptionLook.auto_emphasis` (additive, `skip_serializing_if` false, part of `is_plain`), so a saved caption preset can carry it. When it is on, the Polish tab stars new stressed words when it opens, once per word. The app remembers the words it already proposed in `<project home>/emphasis-seen.json`, so a word the person unstars stays unstarred.
- **Punch-ins** are a pair of eased visual splits (decision 0016) that carry a new optional tag, `VisualSplit.tag: punch_in | punch_out` (additive). The zoom-in split sits at the stressed word. It keeps the overrides of the split in force and zooms that layer's crop 6–10 % (8 % by default) about its centre, easing out over 220 ms. The return split sits at the end of the phrase (0.7–2.5 s) with the overrides in force restored, easing in-out over 320 ms. `punch_ins_set` adds punch-ins, removes them by their zoom-in split, or replaces every punch-in of a framing (the per-cut switch). A punch-in is refused when another split falls inside it. They stay rare: the strongest first, never two within 3 s. The follow-cursor never touches tagged splits.
- **Callouts** are suggested, never automatic. The words are scanned for numbers with their unit ("67 seconds"), project terms and the cut's keyword terms, name-like words (all capitals, CamelCase, letters with digits) and on-screen things ("this button", "right here"). A number or a name becomes a label: a text overlay with role `note` in the pill style. A thing becomes an arrow shape that points into the screen panel. A label is placed inside the safe zones and outside the Instagram overlay zones, the caption band, the face layer and, while it shows, the hook band. Both go in through `overlay_add`.
- **Loop**: the unused `Cut.loop_check` becomes the per-cut option: true = this cut loops, false = it does not, null = not set. `cut_loop_set` sets it. The lint `loop_last_frame` (warning, only on cuts set to loop) compares the visible layers' role, crop, place and opacity at the opening's first frame with those at the cut's last frame. `loop_fix` takes one of two forms:
  - `how: layout` gives the last part part one's look. The last part's visual splits in that framing are removed, and its own insets are swapped for copies of the opening's.
  - `how: tail` splits off a frame-grid tail (0.6 s by default) and gives the tail that look. This keeps the last part's zooms.
- **Hold screen** (`holds_find`, which decodes frames at 4 fps): a step between samples is a jump when at least 35 % of the screen region's cells changed and its robust z is 4 or more against this cut's own steps. The camera and other layers' crops are excluded. The exact frame is found by reading every frame between the two samples. A jump that no speech boundary explains needs a sentence that started at least 0.4 s before it, goes on at least 0.5 s after it, and has no sentence end within 0.4 s. Such a jump is offered as a hold on the frame before the change. The hold is applied by the existing `h` machinery (`hold_toggle` → `hold_screen_here`), which holds until the sentence ends and is one undo step.
- Model kinds are unchanged and the model epoch stays 3: every model change is an optional field that is absent on old rows. **The RenderPlan is unchanged**: a cut without punch-ins plans exactly as before. A cut with punch-ins plans eased splits through decision 0016's `/1`.

## Evidence
- Analysis: `emphasis` unit tests (5): a louder word and a drawn-out word; the same speech 25 dB quieter marks the same words; fillers, weak words and flat speech; phrase-final lengthening; levels, syllables and spacing.
- Core: `polish` unit tests (6) and `tests/polish.rs` (4): keywords in one step with automatic emphasis, a punch-in as a tagged pair (undo, refusals, follow-cursor), a punch-in keeps the split in force, and the loop lint with both fixes and an inset.
- App: `tests/polish.rs` (5), on a synthetic project with level frames and a scripted frame reader.
- e2e: `app/ui/tests/e2e/polish.spec.ts`.

## Limits
- Stress is measured on the source's level frames, not on a re-rendered voice mix, so gain envelopes inside a part are not applied.
- "Unrelated" is approximated by "most of the screen changed", so a fast full-screen scroll also counts.
- Callout placement is a first fit. A label's size is estimated from its characters, not shaped.
