What you keep, what you give up

Premiere is a full professional editor, and gmclip doesn’t try to be one. What gmclip does is the part most YouTubers spend their time on: going through a long take, cutting the dead air and retakes, and making a vertical version for Shorts.

You keep

  • Editing by transcript, with word-snapped trims and one-step filler and pause removal
  • Several videos on one timeline, with an audio lane, volume per part and fades
  • Captions, text hooks, lower thirds and end cards from a brand kit
  • YouTube chapters, thumbnails with versions, and 1080p, 1440p and 4K exports at −16 LUFS
  • Keyboard editing with J/K/L, I/O and a ⌘K command palette

You give up

  • Colour grading, effects, keyframed motion graphics and plugins
  • Multicam, proxies for team workflows, and shared projects
  • After Effects and Audition round trips

Vertical without a second edit

In Premiere, Auto Reframe makes a new 9:16 sequence from your edit, a second sequence to keep in step with the first. In gmclip each cut holds both framings. Trim a sentence and the Short and the YouTube version both change; switch between them with one click.

When Premiere is the better pick

  • You do client work, documentaries or anything that needs colour, effects or multicam.
  • You already pay for Creative Cloud and use other Adobe apps.
  • You need Linux support or a signed, mature app. gmclip is version 0.1.0 for macOS and Windows.

Questions

Is there a free version of Premiere Pro?

No. Adobe offers a 7-day trial and subscriptions. Premiere Rush was discontinued. Adobe’s free Premiere mobile app is for editing on a phone.

What about DaVinci Resolve?

Resolve’s free version is a full professional editor and the closest free match to Premiere. gmclip is narrower: it’s built around transcripts, Shorts and AI agents.

Can I open Premiere projects?

No. Open your source media in gmclip and it transcribes it on your machine.
