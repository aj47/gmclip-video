The free version is a full editor, minus the speech tools

Resolve’s free version is remarkable: editing, colour, Fusion effects and Fairlight audio, with no watermark. What it leaves out is the set of tools that talking videos depend on. Transcription is Studio only, and text-based editing, subtitles from audio, speaker detection and IntelliScript all sit on top of it. Smart Reframe, for turning a horizontal edit into a vertical one, is Studio only too.

gmclip is built around exactly those jobs, and it includes them for free:

  • Word-by-word transcription on your machine, with no hour limit
  • Cut by striking words; trims snap to word edges
  • Tighten clears pauses and filler words, and one undo brings them back
  • Burned-in 1–3 word captions with keyword highlights, timed to each cut’s own audio
  • Every cut keeps 9:16 and 16:9, with screen-plus-facecam presets and a crop that follows the action
  • Your own agent (Claude Code, Codex, pi or opencode) finds the moments worth posting

AI agents in both

Resolve Studio 21.1 added an integration for MCP-compatible AI assistants (summary), so an agent can organise media and build edits there too, with a Studio license. gmclip’s MCP server is free, and every feature in the app is one command an agent can run. See editing with Claude Code.

Use them together

The two don’t have to compete. Cut the talking in gmclip, where the transcript makes it fast, then grade or add effects in Resolve when a video needs it. gmclip exports standard video files at up to 4K.

When Resolve is the better pick

  • You need colour grading, effects, motion graphics or a real audio mix.
  • You edit on Linux, or cut films, music videos or anything that isn’t mostly talking.
  • You already own Studio: its transcription and Smart Reframe cover a lot of what gmclip does.
  • You need a mature app with a long track record. gmclip is version 0.1.0.

Questions

Does the free version of DaVinci Resolve have transcription?

No. Audio transcription, text-based editing and subtitles created from audio are Studio features. You can still type subtitles by hand or import an SRT made elsewhere.

Does free DaVinci Resolve add a watermark?

No. The free version exports without a watermark, up to Ultra HD at 60 fps.

Is gmclip a replacement for Resolve?

For cutting talking videos into YouTube uploads and Shorts, yes. For colour, effects and audio post, no; use Resolve for those.
