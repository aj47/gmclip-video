How editing by text works

  1. Open a recording. MP4, MOV, MKV or WebM. gmclip transcribes it on your machine and times every word.
  2. Read and strike. Select words, a sentence or a paragraph and delete it. The cut updates and the picture jumps straight past it.
  3. Tighten. One command removes pauses and filler words such as “um” and “uh”. One undo brings them back.
  4. Fix the words. Correct a misheard name once and it’s fixed everywhere in the captions. Find and replace works across the whole transcript.
  5. Export. Burn in captions if you want them, and export with Reels or YouTube presets.

Details that make text editing hold up

  • Trims snap to word edges, so cuts fall between words, not inside one.
  • Cuts only hide footage. Nothing is deleted, and restoring text brings the picture back exactly.
  • Named versions and a History you can browse, so you can try a tighter edit and go back.
  • Several videos in one project on one timeline, with a voice track paired to a hi-res picture.
  • Search by words or by meaning across every transcript you own.
  • J/K/L, I/O and a ⌘K command palette for when you’d rather use the keyboard.

Questions

What is a text-based video editor?

An editor where you cut video by editing its transcript instead of dragging clips on a timeline. It’s fastest for talking-head video, podcasts, streams, tutorials and interviews.

Do I still get a timeline?

Yes. gmclip has a full timeline with an audio lane, captions, volume and text on their own lanes. Text and timeline edit the same cut.

Which computers does it run on?

macOS 14 or later (Apple silicon and Intel) and Windows 10 (1809) or 11 on x64.
