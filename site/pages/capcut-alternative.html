Built for talking, not templates

CapCut is a general editor with a huge library of effects, templates and trends. gmclip does one job: turning recordings of people talking (streams, podcasts, tutorials, talks) into clips. So the transcript comes first. You read the recording as text, strike what you don’t want, and the video follows. Tighten removes pauses and filler words in one step, free.

  • 9:16 and 16:9 kept on every cut, with screen-plus-facecam presets
  • 1–3 word captions with keyword highlights, bounce, pop and fade, and a style gallery
  • Text hooks, section labels, punch-ins and end cards from your brand kit
  • Reels and YouTube exports at 1080p, 1440p or 4K, at −16 LUFS loudness
  • Review each cut on your phone by scanning a QR code

Your footage and your terms

gmclip has no account and no cloud, so there’s nothing to agree to about your content. In June 2025 CapCut’s terms drew criticism for the broad license they grant over content you upload (TechRadar); CapCut responded that it doesn’t own your work. If you’d rather not weigh that at all, a local open-source editor sidesteps it.

When CapCut is the better pick

  • You edit on your phone or in a browser.
  • You want trending templates, effects, stickers and a music library.
  • You make montage or B-roll-heavy videos rather than talking ones.
  • You need a mature, signed app today. gmclip is version 0.1.0.

Questions

Is there a CapCut alternative with no watermark?

gmclip never adds a watermark, at any resolution.

Is there an open-source CapCut alternative?

gmclip is open source under MIT or Apache-2.0. For general timeline editing, Kdenlive and Shotcut are open source too; gmclip is the one built around transcripts and Shorts.

Does gmclip run on Linux?

Not yet. There are installers for macOS and Windows.
